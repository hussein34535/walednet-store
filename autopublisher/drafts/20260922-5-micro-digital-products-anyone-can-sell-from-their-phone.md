# 5 Micro-Digital Products Anyone Can Sell From Their Phone

I have a friend, let’s call him "James," who is a junior developer. He works a 9-to-5, but in his spare time, he writes snippets of code to automate boring tasks.

He doesn’t have a full software company. He doesn’t have a fancy SaaS. But last month, he earned $500 selling "boring" code to people who don't know how to write it.

We often obsess over building the next billion-dollar app. But the actual, repeatable income for most of us comes from selling **utility**. Micro-digital products are files, templates, and code that solve a specific problem instantly. You can create them in a weekend and sell them in an afternoon from your phone.

Here are five specific, practical micro-products you can build and sell today.

## 1. Mobile-Optimized Notion Templates

Notion is ubiquitous. The market is saturated, but utility is high.

Don't just sell a generic "Student Planner." That is too generic. Build something specific to a niche that is underserved.

**The Product:** A "Freelance Contractor Tracker" for Upwork or Fiverr freelancers.
**The Problem:** Freelancers are terrible at tracking expenses, tax write-offs, and invoice due dates across multiple platforms.
**How to Build It:** Create a Notion workspace on your phone using the mobile app. Set up three main databases: "Invoices," "Expenses," and "Client Profiles." Use the "Link to database" feature to tie them all together visually.
**The Sale:** Take screenshots of the dashboard. Upload them to Gumroad or a simple Notion page link. Price it at $15–$25. It solves a headache that costs them hours of admin time.

## 2. "Done-For-You" Resume Templates

The job market is competitive. ATS (Applicant Tracking Systems) ruin a lot of CVs.

**The Product:** A CV/Resume template specifically designed to bypass automated filters.
**The Problem:** Standard resume templates use heavy graphics or columns that confuse parsing bots.
**How to Build It:** Open Canva or Google Slides on your phone. Design a clean, single-column layout. Ensure your name is the biggest text on the page. Test it by copying the text into a generic text editor to make sure the formatting doesn't break.
**The Sale:** Create a PDF version. Sell it on your social media bio with the hook: "Get past the bots, not just the humans." $10 is a very low barrier to entry for a career-changing tool.

## 3. JSON/CSV Data Sets

Developers hate manual data entry. If you can provide them with data that is slightly "off" or pre-formatted, they will pay.

**The Product:** A list of 100+ "fake" or "seed" data for testing.
**The Problem:** Developers need data to test APIs or frontend forms, but they don't want to type 100 fake users manually.
**How to Build It:** Use an AI chatbot on your phone (like ChatGPT or Claude). Ask it: "Generate a JSON array of 50 fictional users with names, emails, and phone numbers for a US-based testing environment." Copy the output into a `.json` file or `.csv`. Wrap it in a simple PDF or a text file.
**The Sale:** Market it as "Test Data Bundle." A small CSV file that saves a developer hours of time is worth $5. Create bundles (e.g., "E-commerce test data," "Social Media test data") to increase the value.

## 4. Digital Sticker Packs for Chat Apps

This is a niche, but a profitable one for remote workers.

**The Product:** A pack of 30-50 high-quality digital stickers for WhatsApp or Telegram.
**The Problem:** Remote teams need to convey tone and emotion in text-only chat apps. Emojis are okay, but custom stickers feel more personal and professional.
**How to Build It:** Take photos of physical stickers you own, or create simple vector illustrations using a free app like Vectornator. Organize them into folders by category (e.g., "Workplace Drama," "Coffee Break," "Yes/No").
**The Sale:** Package them as a ZIP file. You can host this for free on Dropbox or Google Drive. Link the file in a post where you show off the stickers. Price it at $3–$5.

## 5. Presets and Filters for Lightroom/Photoshop

If you have a good eye for color, you can sell aesthetics.

**The Product:** A "Dark Mode" photo preset for mobile photography.
**The Problem:** Most people struggle to make their phone photos look professional. They look flat and bright.
**How to Build It:** Go to your favorite photography app (like Lightroom Mobile or Snapseed). Import a high-quality photo. Adjust the shadows, contrast, and temperature until it looks "cinematic." Save this as a Preset.
**The Sale:** Export the preset file. Create a simple preview showing "Before" and "After." Sell it on a platform like Creative Market or even as a free download in exchange for an email signup (building your own audience).

### The "No-Code" Sales Strategy

The hardest part is usually the payment processor. You don't want to set up a Stripe account, bank info, and a website just to make $10.

Use a direct link method. Create a simple page (even just a Google Doc or a Notion page) describing the product. Copy the link. Put that link in your social media bio, in a forum signature, or in a direct message.

When someone buys, you simply email them the file. Or, if you are tech-savvy, you can automate the delivery using a bot.
